## Time and Space in Music

Here we see that a change has occurred. In general, arrangement is mostly about change. We change something, and this change somehow affects our work.

### The Non-Linearity of Musical Time

And here it is worth noting a very important point that, well, it is obvious, but few people think about it: time in music is non-linear and non-unidirectional, unlike in life. In life, we kind of move forward in time constantly. In music, it's not only like that, so to speak.

In music, relatively speaking, there are three simultaneous directions:
1. One direction is **in the moment**, when we hear the sound. What is sounding right now is the present in time.
2. In music, there is also the **past**. This is what we have already listened to. In this case, it is our ambient intro. This is what happened in the past.
3. And there is also the **future**. This is what we expect to hear based on what we heard earlier and what we are hearing right now. That is, our listener expectation of what might happen next.

And music is constantly moving in all these directions. That is, its present is constantly changing in the sense that the sound is constantly changing and going somewhere, right? We have a vector directed toward the future. And what happens in the present affects how we perceive that future. And the past also changes. That is, it's not just that we listened to the ambient intro and it stayed somewhere back there. It continues to be part of the new, constantly updating context. Accordingly, the past of the track also changes when we listen to the track in the present, and it is constantly supplemented with new contextual clarifications.

## The Main Principle: Music Must Be Grown, Not Built

Thus, we come to the final principle of arrangement construction today. It boils down to this.

You have often heard it said that a track must be built like a house—that we have a foundation, drums, bass, then harmony, melody, and it all layers vertically, building this structure like a house. But that is merely the external side. It is a one-sided view of arrangement.

When we take into account all the previous points I mentioned, let's summarize:
- We say that arrangement is form, or rather the process of revealing content through form.
- We talk about the connection between the vertical and the horizontal, about space and time being unified in music.
- We talked about musical time being multidirectional. It works both forward and backward.
- And we say that music is born from contradictions, that arrangement is built from how we handle contradictions.

The concluding part is that **music must be grown, not built**. And that is the main principle of arrangement. It must grow.

We took some fragment, threw something together intuitively here, and got some material, some mold. And then we work with it from the perspective of growing a full-fledged tree of a musical organism out of this mold in time and space simultaneously, in the past and future simultaneously, playing with the listener's memory and expectations.

Thus, we approach arrangement not purely formally, not like artisans who know, "I work in such-and-such genre, I need this kind of bass here, and at such-and-such a minute a hat must appear," and so on. No, we do not proceed from external qualities of arrangement, or even random, arbitrary phenomena fixed in culture. We proceed from the internal logic of the material's development, from internal dialectics, from working with contradictions. And thus, our entire arrangement turns out holistic while remaining diverse and rich within itself.

### Dialectical Regularities and Aesthetic Pleasure

Our task as an arranger is to deepen contradictions so that it is as lively and dramatic as possible in a sense, to clash these contradictions. But we must resolve these contradictions because that is what the listener will expect from us. Of course, we can play with this. We might not give the listener everything they expect, but generally speaking, we should.

Because this is the foundation from which a person derives aesthetic pleasure from music—from recognizing dialectical regularities in music that are everywhere. Everything that exists, everything that has being, is born from dialectical contradictions. And a person knows this. They don't know it on a conceptual level, but it is natural and familiar to them. And by recognizing this dialectic in music, these contradictions, observing them, and getting the expected result from this, a person receives aesthetic pleasure.

In this regard, the difference between simple music and advanced, complex, academic music lies solely in whether the composer has enough craft, talent, and imagination to unfold this play of contradictions within their musical material without losing integrity along the way, so that we don't drift into external contradictions, but proceed from the material we actually have.

We have a motif. That's it, contradictions must develop from this motif. We have a key. If we modulate to another key, that modulation must be driven by contradictions within the key we were working in.

## Practical Recommendations and Conclusion

In this sense, my practical recommendation when you are working on an arrangement and get stuck is: you shouldn't think, "Should I place the bass here because it's customary in the genre I'm working in?" or "After the two-minute mark, something definitely needs to appear," and so on. These are all externally non-essential thoughts. They can help you, or they can distract you. They might help you choose the right instrument, or they might clutter the track with unnecessary fuss.

It is much more productive to ask yourself questions regarding contradictions:
- I am inserting a new instrument: what contradiction does it set up? What contradiction does it resolve?
- Or perhaps it deepens a contradiction that was already there?
- Or, for example, you don't know where to move the track next. Look at what contradictions exist in the material you already have, in this loop you can't break out of: where can I develop them, and so on.

If you think like a dialectician within the track, solutions will come to you as organically and naturally as possible for the material you are working with.

And that is my main message today: how to make an arrangement organic and holistic while keeping it rich—or rather, rich and diverse, yet maintaining integrity.
